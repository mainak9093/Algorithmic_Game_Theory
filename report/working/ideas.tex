\subsection*{2026-08-01 --- What the two negative results jointly require}

Taking Theorem~\ref{thm:obstruction} and Proposition~\ref{prop:msw-false}
together, any correct algorithm must be free to (i) move already-allocated
chores between bundles and (ii) give up total cost while doing so. Every
construction that currently works does so by exhibiting a per-agent potential
$\phi$ with $\edgew{\alloc}(i,j) \le \phi_i - \phi_j$, which telescopes along
paths and vanishes around cycles. \emph{Finding such a potential for general
negative dichotomous costs, or proving that none exists, is the problem.} In
Theorem~\ref{thm:binadd} the potential is $\phi_i = u_i$, the count of
universally disliked chores held; in Theorem~\ref{thm:identical} it is
$\phi_i = \cost(\bundle{i})$. What is the right $\phi$ in general?

Worth noting that the existence of such a $\phi$ is not merely sufficient but
close to necessary: if $\optsubsidy \in \set{0,1}^n$ then $\phi_i :=
\optsubsidy_i$ satisfies $\edgew{\alloc}(i,j) \le \phi_i - \phi_j$ by definition
of an envy-free solution. So the search for a potential and the search for a
proof are the same search, and the content is in constructing $\phi$ and the
allocation \emph{together}.

\subsection*{2026-08-03 --- routes surviving the two no-go results}

\Cref{sec:approach4} closes valuation-level encoding and algorithm-level weight
forcing, and Corollary~\ref{cor:no-refinement} closes every rule that selects
inside the utilitarian-optimal set. What remains splits by which of those
failures it avoids.

\textbf{Route M --- iterated matching.} \cite[\S3]{LMS26} develops the
objective-chores analogue of iterated maximum-weight \emph{perfect} matching,
padding with zero-valued dummies so every agent takes one object per round,
which makes the output balanced. The obvious lift to our setting --- each round
assign every agent one remaining chore minimising the total marginal
$\sum_i [\cost_i(\bundle{i} \cup \set{g_i}) - \cost_i(\bundle{i})]$ --- does
\emph{not} give $\pathw{} \le 1$ for non-additive dichotomous costs: it fails on
$13/250$ instances at $n=3,m=4$ and $10/120$ at $n=3,m=5$ (\texttt{rules.py}).
That is diagnostic rather than discouraging, and \cite{LMS26} flags the obstacle
itself: the telescoping needs additivity, because an additive round-$t$ cost is
independent of what came before whereas a dichotomous marginal depends on the
bundle already held. The repair to attempt is a round invariant replacing the
additive domination --- each round's matching minimum-cost \emph{conditioned on
the current bundles}, with marginals non-increasing across rounds, a ``peel the
free chores first'' discipline. \textbf{Status:} the naive lift is refuted; the
conditioned version is untested.

\textbf{Route A --- induct on agents, not items.} Every approach so far inducts
on items. \cite[\S4.3.1]{LMS26} instead maintains a set of active agents, adds
one at a time, and restores two invariants: compensated utility at least
$\lambda$ with $\lambda \le \subsidy_i \le 1$, and an equality path from every
active agent to the set of minimum compensated utility. For chores this is
attractive because a newcomer starts with an empty bundle, which is the
\emph{cheapest} bundle, so the newcomer is the natural sink and the orientation
problem of \Cref{sec:approach1} does not arise. The invariant carried is the
equality graph rather than a subsidy scalar --- which is precisely the extra
information Theorem~\ref{thm:f2} and Proposition~\ref{prop:f4} say a recipient
rule needs. \textbf{Status:} untried, and the most promising of these.

\textbf{Route N --- non-redundancy instead of coverage.} Theorem~\ref{thm:f2}
says the failing placements are exactly the zero-marginal ones, and the
literature already forbids those under the name non-wastefulness. A duplicate is
redundant for its holder by construction, so ask for a \emph{non-redundant}
allocation of the replica set --- which General Yankee Swap is built to maintain
--- and measure how far non-redundancy falls short of coverage. The two are not
equivalent: a duplicate can be redundant for everyone when every other agent
already holds the type or has zero marginal for it. But the residual gap is
narrower than coverage itself and is exactly characterisable. \textbf{Status:}
cheap to test, untested.

\textbf{Route X --- matroid exchange.} As in \Cref{sec:mrf}. Highest ceiling,
highest cost; the parallel-extension step needs a full proof before anything is
built on it.

\textbf{Ranking.} Route A, then Route M's conditioned repair, then Route N,
then Route X. Conjecture~\ref{conj:h1pp} --- the type-ordered peel --- stays
live alongside them and has the best evidence of anything currently open.
