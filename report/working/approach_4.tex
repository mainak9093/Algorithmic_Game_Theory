\section{Approach 4: Encoding Coverage into the Numbers}
\label{sec:approach4}

Corollary~\ref{cor:coverage} reduces Conjecture~\ref{conj:main} to a single
constraint: the replica instance $\hat{I}$ must admit an allocation in which no
agent holds two copies of one type. Applying~\cite{BKNS22} to $\hat{I}$ is legal
but returns an allocation free to violate that. The obvious response is to
change the numbers so that a violation becomes unattractive --- either at the
level of the valuations, by charging for duplicates, or at the level of the
algorithm, by inflating weights so that the wrong recipient is never selected.

This section closes both. They fail for the same reason, isolated in
\Cref{sec:holderblind}.

% --------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{The literal proposal is a no-op}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:w1}
In $\hat{I}$ the marginal value of a second copy of a type is already exactly
$0$, for every agent and every set: if $b$ has type $j$ and $j \in \tau(S)$ then
$\hat{v}_i(S \cup \set{b}) - \hat{v}_i(S) = 0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$\tau(S \cup \set{b}) = \tau(S)$, and $\hat{v}_i$ factors through $\tau$.
\end{proof}

So ``let a first copy carry weight and a second carry none'' is not a
modification of $\hat{I}$; it \emph{is} $\hat{I}$. Remark~\ref{rem:naive}
already records that the other choice, a flat $+1$ per copy, destroys the
transform outright.

The reason this does not help is that zero marginal means \emph{indifferent},
not \emph{averse}. Lemma~\ref{lem:dupfree} is the exact statement of the damage:
a duplicate is envy-neutral, changing no arc of the envy graph at all. An
objective built out of envy comparisons cannot see duplicates, and the dead ends
of Proposition~\ref{prop:deadends} exist because of this rather than in spite of
it --- at the dead end the one legal move is to spend a copy on a duplicate,
which is free. To repel a duplicate rather than merely fail to attract it, the
duplicate must be made \emph{visible}.

% --------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{The separation programme, correctly stated}

Write $d(S) := \abs{S} - \abs{\tau(S)}$ for the number of duplicated copies in
$S$. Call $u = (u_1,\dots,u_n)$ on $\hat{\items}$ a \emph{faithful reweighting}
if
\begin{itemize}
  \item[(D)] each $u_i$ is dichotomous --- $u_i(\emptyset) = 0$, monotone, all
    marginals in $\set{0,1}$ --- so that~\cite{BKNS22} applies to
    $(\hat{\items}, u)$; and
  \item[(F)] $u_i(S) = \hat{v}_i(S)$ for every duplicate-free $S$, so that the
    envy graph of a coverage allocation is unchanged.
\end{itemize}
Say $u$ \emph{separates} if every non-coverage allocation $B$ has
$\max_i \pathw{B}^u(i) \ge 2$.

\begin{observation}[The programme is logically valid]
\label{obs:w0}
If some faithful $u$ separates, then Conjecture~\ref{conj:main} holds for that
instance.
\end{observation}

\begin{proof}
Applying~\cite{BKNS22} to $(\hat{\items},u)$ returns $B$ with
$\pathw{B}^u \in \set{0,1}^n$; separation forces $B$ to be a coverage
allocation; on a coverage allocation (F) gives $u_i(B_k) = \hat{v}_i(B_k)$ for
all $i,k$, so $\pathw{B}^{\hat{v}} = \pathw{B}^u \le 1$; and
Theorem~\ref{thm:replica} hands back a chore partition with per-agent subsidy in
$\set{0,1}$ and total at most $n-1$.
\end{proof}

The idea is therefore not confused --- it is a genuine reduction template. The
rest of this subsection shows it cannot be filled in.

\begin{theorem}[Duplicate budget]
\label{thm:w2}
Every faithful reweighting satisfies $\hat{v}_i(S) \le u_i(S) \le \hat{v}_i(S) +
d(S)$ for all $i$ and all $S \subseteq \hat{\items}$. So the penalty
$f_i := u_i - \hat{v}_i$ is non-negative, vanishes on duplicate-free sets, and
is bounded by one unit per duplicated copy.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $T \subseteq S$ be duplicate-free with $\tau(T) = \tau(S)$, so
$\abs{S \setminus T} = d(S)$ and $\hat{v}_i(T) = \hat{v}_i(S)$. Monotonicity
gives $u_i(S) \ge u_i(T) = \hat{v}_i(T) = \hat{v}_i(S)$ by (F). Adding the
$d(S)$ elements of $S \setminus T$ one at a time raises $u_i$ by at most $1$
each by (D), so $u_i(S) \le u_i(T) + d(S)$.
\end{proof}

The extreme $f_i = d$ is attained, since $u_i = \hat{v}_i + d$ is monotone,
normalised and has marginals in $\set{0,1}$. So \emph{``a duplicate is worth
$+1$ to everybody'' is the strongest legal penalty there is}, and any scaling by
$\varepsilon \notin \set{0,1}$ leaves the dichotomous class and forfeits
\cite{BKNS22} altogether.

% --------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{The strongest uniform penalty is a potential}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:w3}
Let $u_i = \hat{v}_i + \varepsilon d$ for every $i$, with the same $\varepsilon$
and the same $d$ for all agents. Then for every allocation $B$,
\[
  \edgew{B}^u(i,k) \;=\; \edgew{B}^{\hat{v}}(i,k)
  \;+\; \varepsilon\big(d(B_k) - d(B_i)\big),
\]
and consequently
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(1)] every directed cycle has the same weight in both graphs, so $B$ is
    envy-freeable under $u$ if and only if it is under $\hat{v}$: a uniform
    penalty can never \emph{disqualify} an allocation;
  \item[(2)] the agent holding the \emph{most} duplicates has weakly
    \emph{smaller} subsidy and everyone else weakly larger --- the penalty lands
    on the wrong agents;
  \item[(3)] if $d(B_1) = \dots = d(B_n)$ the two graphs are identical, so such
    allocations are invisible to the penalty however large $\varepsilon$ is.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The identity is immediate from the definitions. The correction telescopes to $0$
around any cycle, giving (1), and along a path to endpoint minus start, giving
(2). For (3) all corrections vanish.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Maximally non-coverage, and invisible]
\label{ex:w3}
Take $n = 3$, $m = 3$ and $\cost_i(S) = \abs{S}$ for every $i$, and let $B_i$ be
both copies of $b_i$. Then $d(B_i) = 1$ for every $i$, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:w3}(3) applies; $\hat{v}_i(B_k) = \abs{\tau(B_k)} = 1$ for all
$i,k$, every arc is $0$ and $\pathw{B}^u = (0,0,0)$. Yet
$\Psi(B)_i = \items \setminus \set{a_i}$ gives every chore two owners --- as far
from coverage as it is possible to be. Applying~\cite{BKNS22} to this $u$ may
return exactly this allocation.
\end{example}

\subsection{No reweighting whatsoever separates}

Theorem~\ref{thm:w3} leaves open the asymmetric case, in which different agents
charge for duplicates of different types. That does break the potential
structure and can create positive cycles, so it has to be excluded separately.

\begin{theorem}[No-go]
\label{thm:w4}
There is a negative dichotomous instance on which \emph{no} faithful
reweighting separates: $n = 3$, $m = 2$, $\cost_1 = \cost_2 = \cost_3 =
\abs{\cdot}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The replica has types $a_1,a_2$ with two copies each and
$\hat{v}_i(S) = \abs{\tau(S)}$. Write $D$ for the pair of copies of $b_1$, and
$s_1, s_2$ for the two copies of $b_2$. For $x \in \set{1,2,3}$ let $B^{(x)}$
give $D$ to agent $x$ and one of $s_1,s_2$ to each of the others; each is
non-coverage.

Let $u$ be faithful. The singletons are duplicate-free, so $u_i(s_1) =
u_i(s_2) = 1$ for every $i$ by (F). For $D$ we have $\hat{v}_i(D) = 1$ and
$d(D) = 1$, so Theorem~\ref{thm:w2} gives $u_i(D) = 1 + \delta_i$ with
$\delta_i \in \set{0,1}$ --- and $\delta = (\delta_1,\delta_2,\delta_3)$ is the
\emph{entire} freedom $u$ has on this family. Fixing $x$ and writing
$\set{y,z} = \agents \setminus \set{x}$, the arcs of $B^{(x)}$ are
\[
  \edgew{}(y,x) = \delta_y, \quad \edgew{}(z,x) = \delta_z, \quad
  \edgew{}(x,y) = \edgew{}(x,z) = -\delta_x, \quad
  \edgew{}(y,z) = \edgew{}(z,y) = 0 .
\]

If $\delta_x = 1$: every arc out of $x$ is $-1$, every arc into $x$ is at most
$1$, and the $y \leftrightarrow z$ arcs are $0$. Any cycle uses one arc into $x$
and one out, so has weight at most $0$; any path out of $y$ has weight at most
$\max(0,\delta_y,\delta_z) \le 1$, likewise out of $z$, and $\pathw{}(x) = 0$.
So $B^{(x)}$ survives. If $\delta_x = 0$ and $\delta_y = \delta_z = 0$: every
arc is $0$ and $B^{(x)}$ survives. If $\delta_x = 0$ and $\delta_k = 1$ for some
$k \ne x$: the two-cycle $k \to x \to k$ has weight $\delta_k - \delta_x = 1 >
0$, and $B^{(x)}$ is excluded.

So $B^{(x)}$ is excluded exactly when $\delta_x = 0$ and $\delta_k = 1$ for some
$k \ne x$. Separation demands all three of $B^{(1)},B^{(2)},B^{(3)}$ be
excluded, forcing $\delta_1 = \delta_2 = \delta_3 = 0$ and hence no $\delta_k =
1$ --- a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Machine-verified strengthening]
\label{thm:w4prime}
On the same instance separation fails even if faithfulness is dropped entirely.
Among all $990$ dichotomous valuations on the four replica copies, none of the
$990^3$ triples $(u_1,u_2,u_3)$ excludes all twelve non-coverage allocations of
the shape ``one duplicated pair plus two singletons''.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Exhaustive, by \texttt{dupsep.py}: collapsing the $990$ valuations by their
behaviour on the critical family leaves $30$ classes, and $0$ of the resulting
class-triples survive the filter. No heuristics are used. The same script also
sweeps the separable $P$-family $u_i(S) = \hat{v}_i(S) + \sum_{j \in P_i}
(\abs{S_j}-1)^{+}$, which for $n = 3$ is the whole separable penalty family, and
finds no separating member on $n=3,m=2$ (all $64$ triples), on $n=3,m=3$ with
unit costs (all $512$), or on the witness of Theorem~\ref{thm:obstruction} (all
$512$).
\end{proof}

The instance of Theorem~\ref{thm:w4} is \emph{not} a counterexample to
Conjecture~\ref{conj:main}: the coverage allocation
$B = (\set{b_1^{(1)},b_2^{(1)}}, \set{b_1^{(2)}}, \set{b_2^{(2)}})$, i.e.\ the
chore partition $(\emptyset, \set{a_2}, \set{a_1})$, has $\pathw{} = (0,1,1)$
and total $2 = n-1$. What is refuted is the \emph{method}, on the easiest
instance available.

\subsection{The same failure at the algorithm level}
\label{sec:findsink}

The second form of the idea leaves the valuations alone and instead inflates the
weight of the block of copies currently being allocated, intending that
receiving a copy drive the recipient's subsidy to $0$ so that an agent already
holding that type is never chosen again. The diagnosis behind it is right.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:f1}
In any run of the algorithm of~\cite{BKNS22} on $\hat{I}$, the \textsc{Extend}
branch never places a duplicate. Every coverage violation is created by
\textsc{FindSink}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\textsc{Extend} returns a pair $(\rho,k)$ drawn from its loop over
$k \in \agents$ and $\ell \in \Mset(\subsidy)$ satisfying
$v_k(A_\ell \cup \set{g}) - v_k(A_\ell) = 1$, and places $g$ into the bundle
$B_k = A_\ell$. So the receiving bundle has strictly positive marginal for $g$.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:w1} a copy of $b_j$ has marginal $0$ whenever
$j \in \tau(S)$, so the receiving bundle contains no copy of $j$.
\end{proof}

But the lever cannot reach the subroutine that does the damage.

\begin{theorem}[\textsc{FindSink} runs only in the zero-marginal regime]
\label{thm:f2}
Suppose the algorithm reaches its \textsc{FindSink} branch, i.e.\
$(\alloc,\subsidy)$ is not extendable with $g$. Then every
$\ell \in \Mset(\subsidy)$ satisfies
$v_\ell(\bundle{\ell} \cup \set{g}) - v_\ell(\bundle{\ell}) = 0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose some $\ell \in \Mset(\subsidy)$ had marginal $1$, and run
\textsc{Extend}'s loop at $k = \ell$, which is then a feasible choice. It
computes a maximum-weight matching $\rho$ on the complete bipartite graph
between $\agents \setminus \set{\ell}$ and itself with weights $v_i(\bundle{j})$;
maximality against the identity matching gives $\sum_{i \ne \ell}
v_i(A_{\rho(i)}) \ge \sum_{i \ne \ell} v_i(\bundle{i})$. Setting
$\rho(\ell) = \ell$ adds $v_\ell(\bundle{\ell})$ to both sides, so the welfare
test passes and \textsc{Extend} returns $(\rho,\ell)$ --- contradicting
non-extendability.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The recipient's subsidy is exactly preserved]
\label{cor:f3}
Let $s$ be the agent returned by \textsc{FindSink} and $\alloc' =
(\bundle{1},\dots,\bundle{s} \cup \set{g},\dots,\bundle{n})$. Then
$\pathw{\alloc'}(s) = \pathw{\alloc}(s)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
\cite{BKNS22} places $s \in \Mset(\subsidy)$, so Theorem~\ref{thm:f2} gives
$v_s(\bundle{s} \cup \set{g}) = v_s(\bundle{s})$ and every arc out of $s$ is
unchanged. Arcs not incident to $s$ are unchanged, and only arcs into $s$ can
rise. Since $\alloc'$ is envy-freeable it has no positive cycle, so
$\pathw{\alloc'}$ is the maximum weight over \emph{simple} paths; a simple path
leaving $s$ never re-enters $s$ and therefore uses only unchanged arcs.
\end{proof}

Three consequences, each fatal on its own.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The lever multiplies a zero.} The premise --- that receiving a
    copy forces the recipient's subsidy to $0$ --- holds only where the
    recipient's marginal is positive. Theorem~\ref{thm:f2} says that in the
    \textsc{FindSink} branch it is provably $0$, and scaling by any weight leaves
    $0$ where it was. Corollary~\ref{cor:f3} makes it exact: the recipient's
    subsidy does not move at all. Inflation bites only in the \textsc{Extend}
    branch, which by Theorem~\ref{thm:f1} was never broken.
  \item \textbf{A duplicate is \textsc{FindSink}'s safest move.} By
    Lemma~\ref{lem:dupfree} a second copy changes no arc whatsoever, so the
    subsidy vector after the tentative assignment is literally identical, the
    subroutine's guard fails on the first test, and it returns immediately. A
    duplicate is never rejected because there is nothing to detect.
  \item \textbf{The scaling forfeits the guarantee anyway.} With block weights
    the marginals lie in $\set{0,W_j}$ rather than $\set{0,1}$, and the step
    of~\cite{BKNS22} that concludes $\subsidy \in \set{0,1}^n$ from integrality,
    together with the unit path bounds throughout its analysis, is gone. Using
    the lever is not \emph{applying}~\cite{BKNS22}; it is writing a new algorithm
    and owing a new proof.
\end{enumerate}

It is worth recording where the lever \emph{does} bite, because that is why it
looks like it works. Within a block, once some agent has been relieved of $a_j$
while others still hold it, the arcs into the relieved agent from any holder
with marginal $1$ carry the inflated weight, so $\Mset(\subsidy)$ collapses onto
exactly the holders who care --- and those are precisely the agents for whom
\textsc{Extend} applies. The lever keeps \textsc{FindSink} from firing at all.
It runs out exactly when every remaining holder of $a_j$ has marginal $0$: then
nothing is inflated, $\Mset(\subsidy)$ reverts to stale values from earlier
blocks, and an already-relieved agent can sit at the top of it. The minimal
shape is $n = 3$ with a chore of marginal $1$ for one agent and $0$ for the
other two.

\subsection{Why both fail: holder-blindness}
\label{sec:holderblind}

The two failures have one cause, and naming it is the useful output of this
section.

A duplicate penalty bites only through the arcs. The arc effect of agent $k$
charging $+1$ for the duplicates in bundle $B_x$ is $+1$ on
$\edgew{}(k,x)$ --- but the same charge levied by $x$ on her own bundle is $+1$
on $u_x(B_x)$, which is $-1$ on every arc \emph{out of} $x$. The two cancel
around every cycle. So a penalty can disqualify an allocation only if the agent
who \emph{holds} the duplicate does not feel it while somebody else does. But
$u$ is fixed before the allocation is chosen, and it is the allocation that
decides who holds the duplicate. Requiring the holder to be blind for every
possible holder forces the penalty to zero, which is exactly the $\delta$
argument of Theorem~\ref{thm:w4}.

Compare the orientation reversal of \Cref{sec:peel}. There the difficulty was
that the navigation rule of~\cite{BKNS22} sends the item to the agent who must
not receive it. Here the difficulty is that the penalty must be levied on an
agent who cannot be identified in advance. Both are the same species of failure:
\emph{a rule fixed ex ante against a quantity determined ex post}.

\begin{remark}[What this rules out, and what it does not]
\label{rem:w5}
\textbf{Ruled out:} encoding coverage into the valuations of the replica
instance so that~\cite{BKNS22} can be used as a black box. Any such encoding is
a dichotomous reweighting, and Theorem~\ref{thm:w4prime} kills it. Also ruled
out: steering the algorithm of~\cite{BKNS22} by inflating weights, by
Theorem~\ref{thm:f2}. Together these close the whole family \emph{``encode
coverage into the numbers and reuse~\cite{BKNS22} as a black box''} from two
directions.

\textbf{Not ruled out:} coverage-aware \emph{algorithms} --- Conjecture
\ref{conj:h1prime} and the search for a peel rule that never enters a deadlock.
Coverage is a constraint on the allocation, and the right place to enforce a
constraint on the allocation is the procedure that builds it, not the objective
it is scored against.
\end{remark}
