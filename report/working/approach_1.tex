\section{Approach 1: Item-by-Item Insertion}
\label{sec:approach1}

\paragraph{The template.} The algorithm of~\cite{BKNS22} maintains an envy-free
solution $(\alloc^t, \subsidy^t)$ over a partial allocation with
$\subsidy^t \in \set{0,1}^n$, allocates one further good at each step, and
repairs the invariant by permuting whole bundles among the agents. Nothing
already allocated is ever moved between bundles. This section follows that
template on the chore side.

\paragraph{Why it looks promising.} Two of its steps come out \emph{easier} than
for goods.

\begin{theorem}[Free insertion]
\label{thm:freeinsert}
Let $\alloc$ be envy-freeable and suppose $\cost_x(\bundle{x} \cup \set{g}) =
\cost_x(\bundle{x})$ for some agent $x$. Then $Z^x$ --- the allocation with $g$
added to $\bundle{x}$ --- is envy-freeable and $\pathw{Z^x}(i) \le
\pathw{\alloc}(i)$ for every $i$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:arcupdate}, arcs between agents other than $x$ are unchanged,
arcs out of $x$ are unchanged since $\delta_x = 0$, and arcs into $x$ satisfy
$\edgew{Z^x}(i,x) = \edgew{\alloc}(i,x) - \delta_{i,x} \le \edgew{\alloc}(i,x)$.
Every arc weight weakly decreases, so no positive cycle appears and no path
weight rises.
\end{proof}

The corresponding step of~\cite{BKNS22} needs the receiver to be maximally
subsidised, a bundle permutation, and extendability machinery, because for goods
the insertion \emph{raises} the arcs into the receiver. Here it lowers them for
free. So the only case needing work is a chore whose marginal cost is $1$ for
every agent on her own bundle --- the \emph{hard case}.

The second tool survives the approach and is used throughout the report.

\begin{theorem}[Cycle-closing bound]
\label{thm:cyclebound}
For any envy-freeable allocation $\alloc$ and any agent $u$,
\[
  \pathw{\alloc}(u) \;\le\; \max\Big(0, \ \max_{v \ne u}
  \big[\,\cost_v(\bundle{u}) - \cost_v(\bundle{v})\,\big]\Big).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since $\alloc$ is envy-freeable, $\envygraph{\alloc}$ contains no
positive-weight directed cycle
(Theorem~\ref{thm:hs-characterisation}(iii)). Every closed walk decomposes into
simple cycles, so every closed walk also has weight at most $0$; deleting a
closed sub-walk from a walk therefore never decreases its weight, and every walk
starting at $u$ has weight at most that of some \emph{simple} path starting at
$u$. As there are finitely many simple paths, the supremum defining
$\pathw{\alloc}(u)$ is attained, by a simple path $P^{\ast}$.

If $P^{\ast}$ is empty then $\pathw{\alloc}(u) = 0$ and the claim holds, the
right-hand side being non-negative. Otherwise $P^{\ast}$ is simple and so does
not revisit $u$; its terminal vertex $v$ therefore satisfies $v \ne u$, and
appending the arc $(v,u)$ yields a genuine simple cycle $C$. By
Theorem~\ref{thm:hs-characterisation}(iii), $\edgew{\alloc}(C) \le 0$, so
\[
  \pathw{\alloc}(u) \;=\; \edgew{\alloc}(P^{\ast})
  \;=\; \edgew{\alloc}(C) - \edgew{\alloc}(v,u)
  \;\le\; -\edgew{\alloc}(v,u)
  \;=\; \cost_v(\bundle{u}) - \cost_v(\bundle{v}),
\]
which is at most the maximum over $v \ne u$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The bound is not an evaluation of $\pathw{\alloc}$]
\label{rem:cyclebound-lossy}
$\pathw{\alloc}(u)$ \emph{is} the maximum path weight out of $u$; the bound is a
one-directional estimate of it in terms of a single arc, discarding everything
about the heaviest path except where it ends. The two are not equivalent and the
inequality is routinely strict: a path $u \to a \to b$ of weight $2$ with
$\edgew{\alloc}(b,u) = -3$ closes a cycle of weight $-1 \le 0$, so
$\pathw{\alloc}(u) = 2$ while the bound returns $3$. Checked against the true
longest-path computation over $172{,}484$ (agent, allocation) pairs, the bound
holds without exception but is exact on only $70.9\%$ of them, with slack up to
$5$ (\texttt{updates/update\_46/cyclebound\_check.py}). Evaluating $\pathw{\alloc}$
requires the longest-path computation; the bound costs $O(n)$ arc lookups and is
used only where an upper estimate suffices ---
Corollary~\ref{cor:onestep} and Theorem~\ref{thm:smallbundle}. Where it was
pressed into service as an equality it failed, which is the content of
Proposition~\ref{prop:closing-arc-lossy}.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:onestep}
If $\cost_v(\bundle{u}) \le \cost_v(\bundle{v}) + 1$ for all $u,v$ --- no agent
would suffer more than one extra unit by taking anybody else's bundle --- then
$\optsubsidy \in \set{0,1}^n$.
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{Where it fails.} One instance kills the template.

\begin{theorem}[The insertion template cannot be completed]
\label{thm:obstruction}
There is a three-agent instance with negative dichotomous valuations, an
envy-freeable partial allocation $\alloc$ with $\optsubsidy \in \set{0,1}^3$, and
an unallocated chore $g$, such that for \emph{every} agent $x$ the allocation
$Z^x$ requires a subsidy of $2$ --- even though the full instance admits an
allocation with subsidy $0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take $\items = \set{a_1, a_2, g}$ with
\[
  \cost_1(S) = \max\big(0, \abs{S} - 1\big), \qquad
  \cost_2(S) = \cost_3(S) = \abs{S},
\]
all dichotomous. Let $\alloc = (\set{a_1,a_2}, \emptyset, \emptyset)$, whose envy
matrix
\[
  \big(\edgew{\alloc}(i,j)\big) =
  \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 1 \\ -2 & 0 & 0 \\ -2 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}
\]
has no positive cycle and gives $\optsubsidy = (1,0,0)$: a legal state. The
marginal cost of $g$ is $1$ for every agent on her own bundle, so this is the
hard case, and inserting $g$ into each bundle in turn gives minimum subsidy
vectors
\[
  Z^1 \mapsto (2,0,0), \qquad Z^2 \mapsto (2,1,0), \qquad Z^3 \mapsto (2,0,1),
\]
each with an entry equal to $2$. Yet the complete allocation
$(\set{a_1}, \set{a_2}, \set{g})$ has an identically zero envy matrix and
subsidy $(0,0,0)$.
\end{proof}

Note that $\cost_1$ is \emph{supermodular}, which is legal --- neither~\cite{BKNS22}
nor this report restricts to submodular valuations --- and the instance genuinely
needs it.

\begin{remark}[Verdict]
\label{rem:deadend}
Any proof of Conjecture~\ref{conj:main} must be allowed to \textbf{re-open
already-allocated bundles}. Allocating one item at a time, never moving an
allocated item, and repairing only by permuting whole bundles is provably
insufficient. This is a genuine asymmetry between the goods and chore problems,
not a gap in the analysis, so effort spent strengthening the insertion invariant
is wasted. The same failure recurs in two further coordinate systems
(\Cref{sec:approach3}, \Cref{sec:insertion-fails-goods}).
\end{remark}
