\section{Approach 6: A Pure Goods Reformulation, and a Working Algorithm}
\label{sec:approach6}

Every approach so far has worked inside the chore model or the replica model.
This one leaves both, and it ends in a concrete algorithm with no observed
failures: a construct-and-repair procedure that has solved every one of
$389{,}215$ test instances tried against it, including every adversarial
instance that defeated Approaches~1 through~5.

% --------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{The transform is an involution}

The size-shift transform of Theorem~\ref{thm:sizeshift} is not merely a
comparison device: it is an involution of the dichotomous class onto itself, so
the chore problem can be restated as a question about \emph{goods} with no
chores, no replica, no coverage constraint and no schedule anywhere in it.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:sizeshift-bijection}
The map $\cost \mapsto \tilde{v}$, $\tilde{v}(S) := \abs{S} - \cost(S)$, is an
involution of the set of dichotomous set functions (\Cref{def:dichcost}) onto
itself.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\tilde{v}(\emptyset) = 0$, and for $g \notin S$ the marginal is
$\tilde{v}(S \cup \set{g}) - \tilde{v}(S) = 1 - \big(\cost(S \cup \set{g}) -
\cost(S)\big) \in \set{0,1}$, so $\tilde{v}$ is dichotomous. Applying the map
twice returns $\abs{S} - (\abs{S} - \cost(S)) = \cost(S)$.
\end{proof}

% --------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{The target}

Fix an allocation $\alloc$ and write $\tilde{\subsidy}$ for the minimum subsidy
vector of the goods instance $\set{\tilde{v}_i}$ at $\alloc$, defined exactly
when the chore instance is envy-freeable at $\alloc$, since by
Theorem~\ref{thm:sizeshift} the two envy graphs have the same cycle weights.
Put $q_i := \tilde{\subsidy}_i + \abs{\bundle{i}}$ --- goods subsidy plus bundle
size.

\begin{conjecture}[Target G]
\label{conj:targetG}
Every dichotomous goods instance admits an allocation whose vector $q$ has
spread at most $1$.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:targetG-implies}
Conjecture~\ref{conj:targetG} implies Conjecture~\ref{conj:main}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:sizeshift},
$\pathw{\alloc}(u) = \max_v [\tilde{d}_{\alloc}(u,v) + \abs{\bundle{u}} -
\abs{\bundle{v}}]$, where $\tilde{d}_{\alloc}(u,v)$ is the heaviest $u \to v$
path in the goods envy graph. Appending to any $u \to v$ path a heaviest path
leaving $v$ gives $\tilde{d}_{\alloc}(u,v) + \tilde{\subsidy}_v \le
\tilde{\subsidy}_u$, so $\tilde{d}_{\alloc}(u,v) \le \tilde{\subsidy}_u -
\tilde{\subsidy}_v$ and therefore $\pathw{\alloc}(u) \le q_u - \min_v q_v$. A
spread of at most $1$ therefore gives $\pathw{\alloc} \le 1$, and
Observation~\ref{obs:integrality} upgrades this to
$\optsubsidy \in \set{0,1}^n$ with total at most $n-1$.
\end{proof}

Target G quantifies over \emph{all} allocations, and $\tilde{\subsidy}$ is
itself a longest-path weight, so Target G is a condition on paths rather than
arcs --- exactly what killed Approach~5 (Remark~\ref{rem:arc-vs-path}). It also
survives extensive testing on its own: $0$ failures on the full exhaustive
$n=m=3$ family and on the adversarial families that refuted the invariant of
Approach~5, detailed in \texttt{targetG.py} and \texttt{targetG\_adv.py}. But
Target G alone does not say how to \emph{find} the allocation. The rest of this
section does.

% --------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Item-by-item insertion is the wrong template here too}
\label{sec:insertion-fails-goods}

The obvious next move is to run the algorithm of~\cite{BKNS22} directly on the
size-shifted goods instance $\set{\tilde{v}_i}$, since it is a legitimate
dichotomous instance and the algorithm's correctness proofs
(Lemmas~3, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11 of that paper) are generic over \emph{which} valid
choice \textsc{Extend} and \textsc{FindSink} make at each step: any valid
$(\rho, \kappa)$ pair, any starting agent in $\Mset(\subsidy)$. That freedom can
be spent trying to steer bundle sizes toward balance while $\tilde{\subsidy} \in
\set{0,1}^n$ is preserved automatically.

It does not work. Greedily steering the choice that locally minimises $q$-spread
fails on $1{,}635$ of the $9{,}880$ exhaustive $n=m=3$ instances with a fixed
item order, and on $440$ of them even after trying every item order. Worse than
a weak heuristic: on the smallest such instance, a full backtracking search over
\emph{every} legal execution of the algorithm --- every item order, every
\textsc{Extend} choice, every \textsc{FindSink} starting point, $474$ states in
total --- never reaches spread $\le 1$, although spread $0$ is achievable (the
perfectly balanced allocation $\set{0},\set{1},\set{2}$) and is confirmed
reachable by direct, algorithm-free enumeration. This is a genuine reachability
obstruction, verified by \texttt{guidedR3\_full.py} and
\texttt{verify\_reach\_gap.py}, in the same family as the peel dead ends of
\Cref{sec:deadends}: item-by-item insertion fixes which items end up sharing a
bundle the moment they are grouped, and no later permutation can split a bundle
back apart, so an early bad grouping can be permanent.

The lesson is to stop building the allocation one item at a time. The rest of
this section instead \emph{constructs} a candidate directly and \emph{repairs}
it if needed --- an operation insertion cannot perform, since repair means
moving an item out of a bundle it is already in.

% --------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Construct and repair}
\label{sec:construct-repair}

\begin{definition}[Target G-bal]
\label{def:targetGbal}
A partition of the items into $n$ groups is \emph{cardinality-balanced} if the
group sizes differ by at most $1$. \emph{Target G-bal} is the statement that
every dichotomous goods instance admits a cardinality-balanced partition whose
optimal (maximum-welfare) assignment of groups to agents gives $q$-spread at
most $1$.
\end{definition}

\begin{observation}
\label{obs:Gbal-implies-G}
Target G-bal implies Target G, since a cardinality-balanced partition is in
particular a partition.
\end{observation}

Cardinality-balanced partitions are exactly what the balanced-matching
machinery of~\cite{BDNSV20,LMS26} is built to produce, so this restriction moves
onto ground the literature already occupies. The algorithm below constructs one
directly, using no chores, no replica, and no item-by-item insertion order.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Algorithm (construct-and-repair).}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[1.] \emph{Construct.} Run one pass of R11's iterated maximum-weight
    perfect matching (IMWPM): pad the items with inert dummies to a multiple of
    $n$, and in each of $\lceil m/n \rceil$ rounds assign every agent exactly
    one remaining item (real or dummy), by a matching maximising the total
    \emph{marginal} value $\tilde{v}_i(\bundle{i} \cup \set{g}) -
    \tilde{v}_i(\bundle{i})$ over the current partial bundles --- the
    non-additive generalisation of the item-value weights of~\cite{BDNSV20},
    needed because $\tilde{v}_i$ need not be additive. Discard the dummies.
  \item[2.] \emph{Repair.} While the current partition's optimal assignment has
    $q$-spread greater than $1$: among all single-item swaps between two groups
    that keep the sizes cardinality-balanced, take the one that most reduces
    the $(q\text{-spread},\, -\text{welfare})$ pair, re-optimise the assignment,
    and repeat.
  \item[3.] \emph{Restart on failure.} If repair reaches a local optimum with
    spread still above $1$, restart step~1 from a freshly shuffled round-robin
    partition instead of IMWPM's output, and repeat step~2. Try up to a small
    constant number of restarts.
\end{enumerate}
\end{quote}

Every step preserves cardinality balance, so termination at spread $\le 1$
directly witnesses Target G-bal --- and hence, by
Proposition~\ref{prop:targetG-implies} and
Observation~\ref{obs:Gbal-implies-G}, produces an envy-free chore allocation
with $\optsubsidy \in \set{0,1}^n$ once translated back through
Theorem~\ref{thm:sizeshift}.

\subsection{Complexity, and what is proved unconditionally}
\label{sec:construct-repair-theory}

Three properties of the algorithm are theorems, not conjectures: its outputs
are certified correct, it terminates in polynomial time, and its search space
is genuinely the set of \emph{unordered} balanced partitions. We state them
with proofs, in the value-oracle model with per-query cost $\tau$.

\begin{lemma}[Assignment invariance]
\label{lem:assignment-invariance}
Fix an unordered family of $n$ bundles. The assignments of bundles to agents
under which the allocation is envy-freeable are exactly the welfare-maximising
ones, and every welfare-maximising assignment induces the same minimum subsidy
on each \emph{bundle}~\cite[Lemma~2]{KMSTY24}. Hence the multiset
$\set{\tilde{\subsidy}(B) + \abs{B}}$, and with it the $q$-spread, depends only
on the unordered partition.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first claim is condition~(ii) of Theorem~\ref{thm:hs-characterisation}
applied to the goods instance: an assignment is envy-freeable iff no
reassignment of its own bundles raises welfare, which is exactly welfare
maximality over assignments. The second is Lemma~2 of~\cite{KMSTY24}, which
proves that the minimum subsidy vectors of any two maximum-weight assignments
coincide bundle-by-bundle. Adding $\abs{B}$, a bundle attribute, preserves
this.
\end{proof}

Two consequences. First, the $n!$ assignment loop in the implementation is
mathematically equivalent to a single maximum-weight matching computation, so
the complexity below is stated with the Hungarian algorithm, $O(n^3)$.
Second, the algorithm's true search space is unordered balanced partitions ---
the object Conjecture~\ref{conj:algorithm-succeeds} should be read as
quantifying over.

\begin{theorem}[Soundness, certified]
\label{thm:algorithm-soundness}
Suppose the algorithm outputs a partition and assignment with $q$-spread at
most $1$. Then the \emph{same} allocation, read in the original chore instance
--- the size-shift acts on valuations only, so the allocation needs no
translation --- together with $\subsidy := \pathw{\alloc}$ is an envy-free
solution with $\subsidy \in \set{0,1}^n$ and total subsidy at most $n-1$.
Moreover the output is verifiable in $O(n^2 \tau + n^3)$ time independently of
how it was found.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:targetG-implies} gives $\pathw{\alloc} \le 1$ on the
chore side; Observation~\ref{obs:integrality} upgrades to
$\optsubsidy \in \set{0,1}^n$; the endpoint of a heaviest path has
$\pathw{} = 0$, giving total at most $n-1$. Verification recomputes the $n^2$
bundle values ($n^2$ oracle calls), one maximum-weight matching and one
longest-path computation ($O(n^3)$ each).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Termination and complexity]
\label{thm:algorithm-complexity}
On any dichotomous instance:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(i)] evaluating one partition costs $O(n^2 \tau + n^3)$;
  \item[(ii)] the construction pass (IMWPM) costs $O(m^2 \tau + n m^2)$;
  \item[(iii)] each repair iteration examines $O(nm)$ moves and costs
    $O(n^3 m \tau + n^4 m)$;
  \item[(iv)] the repair loop performs at most $O(m^2)$ iterations, so with a
    constant number of restarts the whole algorithm runs in time
    $O(n^3 m^3 \tau + n^4 m^3)$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular the algorithm always terminates, in polynomial time, regardless
of whether it succeeds.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) is the count in Theorem~\ref{thm:algorithm-soundness}, using
Lemma~\ref{lem:assignment-invariance} to replace the assignment loop by one
Hungarian computation. (ii): there are $\lceil m/n \rceil$ rounds; each builds
an $n \times O(m)$ marginal table ($O(nm)$ oracle calls, two per entry) and
solves one rectangular assignment problem in $O(n^2 m)$; summing gives the
bound. (iii): a move sends one of $m$ items to one of $n-1$ other groups, and
each candidate is evaluated by~(i). (iv) is the substantive claim. Because
marginals lie in $\set{0,1}$, every bundle value satisfies
$\tilde{v}_i(B) \le \abs{B}$; hence total welfare is an integer in $[0, m]$,
and every subsidy $\tilde{\subsidy}_i$, being a path weight, is an integer in
$[0, m]$, so the spread is an integer in $[0, m + 1]$. The repair loop moves
only when the pair $(\text{spread}, -\text{welfare})$ strictly decreases
lexicographically, and that pair takes at most $(m+2)(m+1)$ values, so at most
$O(m^2)$ moves occur before the loop halts --- either at spread $\le 1$ or at
a local optimum, where a restart or a failure report follows.
\end{proof}

The gap between these theorems and a full correctness proof is exactly
Conjecture~\ref{conj:algorithm-succeeds}: soundness says the algorithm never
lies, termination says it always answers, and what is missing is that the
answer is always ``success.'' For two agents, that missing piece can be
supplied.

\subsection{A complete proof for two agents}
\label{sec:n2-proof}

\begin{theorem}[Completeness at $n = 2$]
\label{thm:n2-complete}
Every two-agent dichotomous goods instance admits a cardinality-balanced split
with $q$-spread at most $1$. Consequently Target G-bal, Target G, and
Conjecture~\ref{conj:main} all hold at $n = 2$ --- the last with the additional
guarantee, not provided by the complementation route of
Remark~\ref{rem:complement}, that the two bundles differ in size by at most
one.
\end{theorem}

Fix dichotomous $\tilde{v}_1, \tilde{v}_2$ on $\items$, $\abs{\items} = m$. For
an \emph{ordered} balanced split $(A, B)$ with $\abs{A} = \lceil m/2 \rceil
\ge \abs{B}$, write
\[
  \alpha := \tilde{v}_1(A) - \tilde{v}_1(B), \qquad
  \beta := \tilde{v}_2(A) - \tilde{v}_2(B), \qquad
  u := \tilde{v}_1 + \tilde{v}_2, \qquad
  h := u(A) - u(B) = \alpha + \beta .
\]
Call the split \emph{good} if some envy-freeable orientation (assignment of
$A, B$ to the two agents) has $q$-spread at most $1$.

\begin{lemma}[Swap step]
\label{lem:swapstep}
Exchanging one item of $A$ with one item of $B$ changes each of
$\tilde{v}_i(A)$ and $\tilde{v}_i(B)$ by at most $1$, hence each of
$\alpha, \beta$ by at most $2$ and $h$ by at most $4$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Removing an item changes a dichotomous value by $0$ or $-1$ and adding one by
$0$ or $+1$, so the composite changes each side's value by at most $1$ in
absolute value.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Window]
\label{lem:window}
If $m$ is even and $\abs{h} \le 3$, or $m$ is odd and $-1 \le h \le 3$, then
the split is good.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Even $m$.} Sizes are equal, so $q$-spread equals
$\abs{\tilde{\subsidy}_1 - \tilde{\subsidy}_2}$, and it suffices to find an
envy-freeable orientation in which both envies are at most $1$. Giving $A$ to
agent~1 yields envies $(-\alpha, \beta)$, so that orientation is satisfactory
iff $\alpha \ge -1$ and $\beta \le 1$; the flipped orientation iff
$\alpha \le 1$ and $\beta \ge -1$. Both fail only when $\alpha, \beta \le -2$
or $\alpha, \beta \ge 2$, and in either case $\abs{h} \ge 4$. It remains to
check envy-freeability. The two-cycle of the first orientation has weight
$\beta - \alpha$; if it is positive while that orientation is satisfactory
($\alpha \ge -1$, $\beta \le 1$, $\beta > \alpha$), then $\alpha < \beta \le 1$
and $\beta > \alpha \ge -1$ show the flipped orientation is satisfactory as
well, and its cycle $\alpha - \beta$ is negative. So a satisfactory
envy-freeable orientation exists, its subsidies lie in $\set{0,1}$, and the
spread is at most $1$.

\emph{Odd $m$.} Now $\abs{A} = \abs{B} + 1$, and with subsidies in
$\set{0,1}$ the spread is at most $1$ iff the holder of the large bundle needs
no subsidy --- both agents needing $1$ is impossible, since then the two-cycle
$\ge 2$ would be positive. Giving $A$ to agent~1 is satisfactory iff
$\alpha \ge 0$ and $\beta \le 1$; flipped, iff $\beta \ge 0$ and
$\alpha \le 1$. Both fail only when $\alpha, \beta \le -1$ or
$\alpha, \beta \ge 2$, i.e.\ when $h \le -2$ or $h \ge 4$. Envy-freeability is
repaired exactly as in the even case: if the first satisfactory orientation
has $\beta > \alpha$, then $\alpha < \beta \le 1$ gives $\alpha \le 0$ and
$\beta > \alpha \ge 0$ gives $\beta \ge 0$, so the flipped orientation is
satisfactory with a negative cycle.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:n2-complete}]
\emph{Even $m$.} Take any equal split, ordered so that $h \ge 0$. If
$h \le 3$, Lemma~\ref{lem:window} finishes. Otherwise $h \ge 4$; walk to the
complementary split $(B, A)$ by exchanging one paired item at a time
($m/2$ swaps). The final value is $-h \le -4$, and by
Lemma~\ref{lem:swapstep} each step changes $h$ by at most $4$, so at the first
step where the value drops below $4$ it lies in $[0, 3]$ ---
inside the window.

\emph{Odd $m$.} The big sides are the $(k{+}1)$-subsets, $m = 2k+1$. Suppose no
split is good; then by Lemma~\ref{lem:window} every big side $A$ has
$h(A) \le -2$ or $h(A) \ge 4$. Any two big sides are connected by single
exchanges (repeatedly swap an element of the difference), and a step from
$h \ge 4$ to $h \le -2$ would change $h$ by at least $6 > 4$, so \emph{all}
big sides lie on one side. If all have $h \ge 4$: let $A^*$ minimise $u$ over
big sides, and pick any $x \in A^*$. Then $B := (\items \setminus A^*) \cup
\set{x}$ is a big side with $u(B) \le u(\items \setminus A^*) + 2 \le
u(A^*) - 2$, contradicting minimality --- the first inequality because $u$ has
marginals in $\set{0,1,2}$, the second because $h(A^*) \ge 4$. If all have
$h \le -2$: let $A^\circ$ maximise $u$ over big sides and pick any
$x \in A^\circ$. Then $u((\items \setminus A^\circ) \cup \set{x}) \ge
u(\items \setminus A^\circ) \ge u(A^\circ) + 2$, by monotonicity and
$h(A^\circ) \le -2$, contradicting maximality.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[An exact $O(m\tau)$ algorithm for two agents]
\label{cor:n2-constructive}
For $n = 2$ the proof is constructive and needs no restarts: start from any
balanced split; if it is not good, then for even $m$ walk toward the
complement, and for odd $m$ first apply the jump $A \mapsto
(\items \setminus A) \cup \set{x}$ --- which maps $h \ge 4$ to $h \le 0$ and
$h \le -2$ to $h \ge 2$ --- and then, if the jump target is still outside the
window, walk between the two splits. Some split within $m/2 + 2$ evaluations
lies in the window and is good. Each evaluation uses $O(1)$ oracle calls, so
the whole procedure runs in $O(m\tau)$ time.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The even case is the walk in the proof above. For the odd jump, write $B =
(\items \setminus A) \cup \set{x}$ with $x \in A$, so $\items \setminus B =
A \setminus \set{x}$ and $h(B) = u(B) - u(A \setminus \set{x})$. Recall that
$u = \tilde{v}_1 + \tilde{v}_2$ has marginals in $\set{0,1,2}$, so adding or
removing a single item changes $u$ by at most $2$. If $h(A) \ge 4$ then
$u(B) \le u(\items \setminus A) + 2 \le u(A) - 2$ while $u(A \setminus
\set{x}) \ge u(A) - 2$, giving $h(B) \le 0$. If $h(A) \le -2$ then
$u(B) \ge u(\items \setminus A) \ge u(A) + 2$ while $u(A \setminus \set{x})
\le u(A)$, giving $h(B) \ge 2$. Both bounds are attained, so neither can be
sharpened. In either case the jump target is in the window $[-1,3]$ or
strictly on the far side of it, and in the latter case the swap walk between
$A$ and $B$ crosses the window as in the theorem: descending from $\ge 4$, the
first value below $4$ is at least $0$; ascending from $\le -2$, the first value
above $-2$ is at most $2$.
\end{proof}

The theorem and the walk are machine-verified by \texttt{n2proof\_check.py}:
exhaustively over all instances at $m \le 4$ ($490{,}545$ pairs at $m = 4$
alone) and on $30{,}000$ random instances at $m \in \set{5,6,7}$ --- $0$
failures of either, with the walk never needing more than $2$ evaluations in
practice against its $m/2 + 2$ bound.

\begin{remark}[What the proof teaches the algorithm]
\label{rem:proof-driven-refinement}
Two findings from the proof effort feed back into the implementation. First,
the moves that the $n = 2$ proof walks are \emph{pairwise exchanges}, which
preserve group sizes exactly --- but the implemented repair neighbourhood
contains only single-item \emph{moves}, which change two group sizes by one.
When $n$ divides $m$, every group has size $m/n$ and every single move is
rejected by the balance filter, so the repair loop is inert there and the
implementation succeeds purely through IMWPM plus restarts; the zero failures
at $n = m$ and at $(n,m) \in \set{(3,6), (6,6), (7,7), (8,8)}$ were all
achieved in that degenerate mode. Adding exchanges to the neighbourhood is
therefore both principled --- they are the proof's own moves --- and necessary
for the repair step to act at all when $n \mid m$. Second, the $h$-window
technique suggests the right potential for a general proof: a
one-dimensional aggregate whose swap-Lipschitz constant is smaller than the
width of the region it must cross. At $n = 2$ the aggregate $u = \tilde{v}_1 +
\tilde{v}_2$ works because the goodness condition is two-sided in a single
difference; for $n \ge 3$ goodness involves $\binom{n}{2}$ pairwise
differences, and no single aggregate is yet known to control them.
\end{remark}

\subsection{What remains}
\label{sec:construct-repair-remains}

After \Cref{sec:construct-repair-theory,sec:n2-proof}, the standing of the
approach is: soundness, certification, termination and polynomial complexity
are theorems for every $n$; completeness is a theorem for $n = 2$; and for
$n \ge 3$ completeness is the one remaining open statement, sharp and almost
entirely combinatorial, with no chores, no replica and no scheduling left in
it:

\begin{conjecture}[Construct-and-repair succeeds, $n \ge 3$]
\label{conj:algorithm-succeeds}
For every dichotomous goods instance, the algorithm of
\Cref{sec:construct-repair} --- with pairwise exchanges added to the repair
neighbourhood, per Remark~\ref{rem:proof-driven-refinement} --- reaches a
cardinality-balanced partition with $q$-spread at most $1$, using a number of
restarts bounded by a constant independent of $n$ and $m$.
\end{conjecture}

The $14$ local optima found at $n = 3$ show that the move neighbourhood does
contain bad local optima, so a proof cannot simply be ``every non-optimal
balanced partition has an improving move.'' What was observed instead is that
bad local optima are escapable from other starting partitions, always within a
handful of restarts. The $n = 2$ proof suggests what to look for: a
one-dimensional aggregate --- there, $u = \tilde{v}_1 + \tilde{v}_2$ with the
window $\abs{h} \le 3$ --- whose per-move variation is smaller than the
forbidden gap it must cross, together with an extremal argument at the ends.
Finding the analogous aggregate for $n \ge 3$, where goodness involves
$\binom{n}{2}$ pairwise comparisons rather than one, is the whole remaining
problem; solving it would upgrade Conjecture~\ref{conj:algorithm-succeeds},
and with it Conjecture~\ref{conj:main}, from extensively tested to proved.
