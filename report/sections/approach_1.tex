\section{Approach 1: Item-by-Item Insertion}
\label{sec:approach1}

The algorithm of~\cite{BKNS22} maintains an envy-free solution
$(\alloc^t, \subsidy^t)$ over a partial allocation with
$\subsidy^t \in \set{0,1}^n$, and at each step allocates one further good,
repairing the invariant by permuting whole bundles among the agents when
necessary. Nothing already allocated is ever moved between bundles. This section
follows that template on the chore side. Two of its steps come out
\emph{easier} than in~\cite{BKNS22}; the template nevertheless cannot be
completed, and \Cref{sec:obstruction} proves it.

Throughout, $\alloc$ is a partial allocation, $g \notin \bigcup_i \bundle{i}$ is
an unallocated chore, and $Z^x$, $\delta_x$, $\delta_{i,x}$ are as in
\Cref{sec:arcupdate}.

% --------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{The easy case is easier than for goods}

\begin{theorem}[Free insertion]
\label{thm:freeinsert}
Let $\alloc$ be envy-freeable and suppose $\delta_x = 0$ for some agent $x$,
that is, $\cost_x(\bundle{x} \cup \set{g}) = \cost_x(\bundle{x})$. Then $Z^x$ is
envy-freeable and $\pathw{Z^x}(i) \le \pathw{\alloc}(i)$ for every $i$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:arcupdate}, arcs between agents other than $x$ are unchanged;
arcs out of $x$ satisfy $\edgew{Z^x}(x,j) = \edgew{\alloc}(x,j) + \delta_x =
\edgew{\alloc}(x,j)$; and arcs into $x$ satisfy $\edgew{Z^x}(i,x) =
\edgew{\alloc}(i,x) - \delta_{i,x} \le \edgew{\alloc}(i,x)$. So every arc weight
weakly decreases. No positive-weight cycle can therefore appear, and no path
weight can rise; the claims follow from
Theorems~\ref{thm:hs-characterisation} and~\ref{thm:hs-minsubsidy}.
\end{proof}

It is worth recording what is \emph{absent} from that proof. There is no
hypothesis that $x$ be maximally subsidised, no permutation of bundles, and no
extendability machinery: the corresponding step of~\cite{BKNS22} needs all
three, because for goods the insertion raises the arcs \emph{into} the receiver
and those have to be controlled by placing her in $\Mset(\subsidy)$. Here the
insertion lowers them for free. Consequently:

\begin{observation}
\label{obs:hardcase}
The only case requiring work is a chore whose marginal cost is $1$ for every
agent on her own current bundle: $\delta_x = 1$ for all $x \in \agents$. We call
this the \emph{hard case}.
\end{observation}

% --------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{A cycle-closing bound}

The following is the cheapest general handle on $\pathw{\alloc}$ available, and
it is the chore form of the device~\cite{BDNSV20} uses in its Section~4, where a
lower bound of $-1$ on every arc weight is shown to force a subsidy of at most
one per agent.

\begin{theorem}[Cycle-closing bound]
\label{thm:cyclebound}
For any envy-freeable allocation $\alloc$ and any agent $u$,
\[
  \pathw{\alloc}(u) \;\le\; \max\Big(0, \ \max_{v \ne u}
  \big[\,\cost_v(\bundle{u}) - \cost_v(\bundle{v})\,\big]\Big).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The empty path contributes $0$. Let $P$ be a nonempty path from $u$ to some
$v \ne u$. Appending the arc $(v,u)$ closes $P$ into a directed cycle, whose
weight is at most $0$ because $\alloc$ is envy-freeable
(Theorem~\ref{thm:hs-characterisation}). Hence
$\edgew{\alloc}(P) \le -\edgew{\alloc}(v,u) = \cost_v(\bundle{u}) -
\cost_v(\bundle{v})$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:onestep}
If $\cost_v(\bundle{u}) \le \cost_v(\bundle{v}) + 1$ for all $u,v \in \agents$
--- no agent would suffer more than one extra unit by taking anybody else's
bundle --- then $\optsubsidy \in \set{0,1}^n$.
\end{corollary}

The hypothesis is sufficient and far from necessary: an agent with
$\cost_i \equiv 0$ can absorb everything, which breaks the hypothesis while the
subsidy stays $0$. It is nevertheless what does the work in
Theorem~\ref{thm:binadd}, where the telescoping bound $\edgew{\alloc}(i,j) \le
u_i - u_j$ supplies exactly this.

\subsection{The obstruction}
\label{sec:obstruction}

In the hard case, Lemma~\ref{lem:arcupdate} says every arc leaving the receiving
agent $x$ rises by exactly $1$. Since a path starting at $x$ then rises by $1$,
the condition $\subsidy_x = 0$ is \emph{necessary}. The following theorem shows
that no condition of this kind can be sufficient.

Say that $(\alloc, \subsidy)$ is \emph{extendable with $g$} if there is a
permutation $\sigma$ such that $\alloc_\sigma$ is envy-freeable and some agent
$\kappa$ has $\cost_\kappa(A_{\sigma(\kappa)} \cup \set{g}) =
\cost_\kappa(A_{\sigma(\kappa)})$ --- that is, if after some welfare-preserving
reassignment of the existing bundles, somebody can take $g$ for free and
Theorem~\ref{thm:freeinsert} applies.

\begin{theorem}[The insertion template cannot be completed]
\label{thm:obstruction}
There is a three-agent instance with negative dichotomous valuations, an
envy-freeable partial allocation $\alloc$ with $\optsubsidy \in \set{0,1}^3$,
and an unallocated chore $g$, such that for \emph{every} agent $x \in \agents$
the allocation $Z^x$ requires a subsidy of $2$ --- even though the full instance
admits an allocation with subsidy $0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take $\items = \set{a_1, a_2, g}$ and
\[
  \cost_1(S) = \max\big(0, \abs{S} - 1\big), \qquad
  \cost_2(S) = \cost_3(S) = \abs{S} .
\]
Each is dichotomous: $\cost_1$ has marginals $0,1,1$ as $\abs{S}$ runs
$0,1,2$, and $\cost_2, \cost_3$ are binary additive. Note that $\cost_1$ is
\emph{supermodular}, which is legal --- neither~\cite{BKNS22} nor the present
paper restricts to submodular valuations --- and the instance genuinely needs
it.

Consider the partial allocation $\alloc = (\set{a_1,a_2}, \emptyset, \emptyset)$.
Its envy graph has weight matrix
\[
  \big(\edgew{\alloc}(i,j)\big)_{i,j} =
  \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 1 \\ -2 & 0 & 0 \\ -2 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix},
\]
which has no positive-weight cycle, and $\optsubsidy = (1,0,0)$. So
$(\alloc, \optsubsidy)$ is a legal state of the invariant.

The marginal cost of $g$ is $1$ for every agent on her own bundle:
$\cost_1(\set{a_1,a_2,g}) - \cost_1(\set{a_1,a_2}) = 2 - 1 = 1$ and
$\cost_i(\set{g}) - \cost_i(\emptyset) = 1$ for $i = 2,3$. So we are in the hard
case. Moreover $(\alloc,\optsubsidy)$ is not extendable with $g$: agent~1 must
keep $\set{a_1,a_2}$ under any envy-freeable reassignment, since that bundle
costs her $1$ but costs agents~2 and~3 two apiece, and no reassignment gives
anybody a bundle on which $g$ is free.

Inserting $g$ into each bundle in turn gives the minimum subsidy vectors
\[
  Z^1 \mapsto (2,0,0), \qquad Z^2 \mapsto (2,1,0), \qquad Z^3 \mapsto (2,0,1),
\]
each of which has an entry equal to $2$. Yet the complete allocation
$(\set{a_1}, \set{a_2}, \set{g})$ has an identically zero envy matrix and
subsidy $(0,0,0)$.
\end{proof}

The failure is not that envy-freeability breaks --- it does not.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:e1}
In the hard case, if $(\alloc, \optsubsidy)$ is not extendable with $g$, then
$Z^x$ is envy-freeable for \emph{every} $x \in \agents$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Cycles avoiding $x$ are unchanged by Lemma~\ref{lem:arcupdate}. Let $C$ be a
cycle through $x$ and let $i$ be its predecessor of $x$. The only arcs of $C$
that change are the one leaving $x$, which rises by $\delta_x = 1$, and the one
entering $x$ from $i$, which falls by $\delta_{i,x}$; hence
$\edgew{Z^x}(C) = \edgew{\alloc}(C) + 1 - \delta_{i,x}$.

If $\edgew{\alloc}(C) \le -1$ then $\edgew{Z^x}(C) \le 0$ immediately. If
$\edgew{\alloc}(C) = 0$, rotating the bundles along $C$ is a reassignment that
preserves total cost, so by Theorem~\ref{thm:hs-characterisation}(ii) the
rotated allocation is again envy-freeable, and in it agent $i$ receives
$\bundle{x}$. Non-extendability therefore forces $\cost_i(\bundle{x} \cup
\set{g}) \ne \cost_i(\bundle{x})$, i.e.\ $\delta_{i,x} = 1$, and again
$\edgew{Z^x}(C) \le 0$. Since $\alloc$ is envy-freeable no cycle has
$\edgew{\alloc}(C) \ge 1$, so these cases are exhaustive.
\end{proof}

So envy-freeability survives every insertion; it is only the $\set{0,1}$ bound
on the subsidy that breaks. The reason is visible in \Cref{sec:arcupdate}. A
path $u \rightsquigarrow i \to x \to y \rightsquigarrow$ of weight $1$ that
merely \emph{passes through} $x$ has its outgoing arc raised by $\delta_x = 1$
and its incoming arc lowered by $\delta_{i,x}$, so it becomes a path of weight
$2$ whenever $\delta_{i,x} = 0$. No condition on $\subsidy_x$ prevents this. In
the instance above both $x = 2$ and $x = 3$ are hit this way by $i = 1$.

Nor can the repair subroutine of~\cite{BKNS22} be mirrored. Its navigation rule
walks to an agent that has just required a subsidy of at least $2$; in the goods
world such an agent is automatically maximally subsidised, which is the property
the argument needs. In the chore world the same agent automatically has
$\subsidy_u = 1$, and is therefore precisely the kind of agent to whom the chore
must \emph{not} be given.

\begin{remark}[What this rules out]
\label{rem:deadend}
Any proof of Conjecture~\ref{conj:main} must be allowed to \textbf{re-open
already-allocated bundles}. The template of~\cite{BKNS22} --- allocate one item
at a time, never move an item that has been allocated, and repair only by
permuting whole bundles --- is provably insufficient, by
Theorem~\ref{thm:obstruction}. This is a genuine asymmetry between the goods and
chore problems and not a gap in the present analysis, so effort spent
strengthening the insertion invariant is effort wasted.
\end{remark}
