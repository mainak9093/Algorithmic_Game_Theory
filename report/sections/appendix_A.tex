\section{Failure of a BKNS-type incremental construction}
\label{app:extension-failure-sec}

\begin{example}[Failure of a BKNS-type incremental extension~\cite{BKNS22}]
\label{app:extension-failure}
Consider three agents $\agents = \set{1,2,3}$ and three chores
$\items = \set{a,b,c}$. We work in cost form, writing $\cost_i = -v_i$ for
agent $i$'s non-negative cost function. Thus an allocation
$\alloc = (\bundle1,\dots,\bundle n)$ together with a subsidy vector
$\subsidy = (\subsidy_1,\dots,\subsidy_n)$ is envy-free if and only if
\[
  \cost_i(\bundle i) - \subsidy_i \;\le\; \cost_i(\bundle j) - \subsidy_j
  \qquad \text{for all } i,j \in \agents .
\]

The cost functions depend only on bundle size:
\[
  \cost_1(S) = \max\set{0,\ \abs S - 1},
  \qquad
  \cost_2(S) = \cost_3(S) = \min\set{\abs S,\ 2}.
\]
Thus
\[
  \begin{array}{c|cccc}
    \abs S & 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 \\
    \hline
    \cost_1(S) & 0 & 0 & 1 & 2 \\
    \cost_2(S) = \cost_3(S) & 0 & 1 & 2 & 2
  \end{array}
\]
Every marginal cost belongs to $\set{0,1}$, so these are dichotomous cost
functions.

Consider the following two steps of an incremental construction.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llcc}
step & allocation & $\optsubsidy$ & $\Mset(\optsubsidy)$ \\
\hline
start & $(\emptyset,\ \emptyset,\ \emptyset)$ & $(0,0,0)$ & $\set{1,2,3}$ \\
$a$ to agent $1$ & $(\set{a},\ \emptyset,\ \emptyset)$ & $(0,0,0)$ & $\set{1,2,3}$ \\
$b$ to agent $1$ & $(\set{a,b},\ \emptyset,\ \emptyset)$ & $(1,0,0)$ & $\set{1}$ \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Both steps are legal: at each one the recipient lies in $\Mset(\optsubsidy)$
and the resulting allocation is envy-freeable with subsidies in $\set{0,1}^3$.

Now $c$ is currently unallocated and the partial allocation is
\[
  X_1 = \set{a,b}, \qquad X_2 = X_3 = \emptyset ,
\]
whose pointwise-minimal subsidy vector is $\optsubsidy = (1,0,0)$. Indeed,
agent $1$ has cost $1$ for her own bundle and cost $0$ for either empty bundle,
so she requires one unit of subsidy; agents $2$ and $3$ have no envy, since
they have cost $0$ for their own empty bundles and cost $2$ for $X_1$.

We claim that the remaining chore $c$ cannot be inserted so that the resulting
allocation admits an envy-free subsidy vector in $\set{0,1}^3$, even if the
existing bundles $\set{a,b}, \emptyset, \emptyset$ may first be permuted among
the agents.

If $c$ is added to the two-chore bundle, the resulting bundle sizes are
$(3,0,0)$. Every agent evaluates a three-chore bundle at cost $2$ and an empty
bundle at cost $0$. Hence the agent receiving the three-chore bundle requires a
subsidy at least two units larger than that of an agent receiving an empty
bundle, and no subsidy vector in $\set{0,1}^3$ can make the resulting
allocation envy-free.

It remains to consider the case in which $c$ is added to an empty bundle. The
resulting bundle sizes are $(2,1,0)$, up to permutation. If an agent in
$\set{2,3}$ receives the two-chore bundle, she evaluates it at cost $2$ and the
empty bundle at cost $0$, again requiring a subsidy difference of at least two.

Therefore the only remaining possibility is that agent $1$ receives the
two-chore bundle. Let $i \in \set{2,3}$ receive the singleton and let $j$
receive the empty bundle. Envy-freeness for agent $1$ with respect to $i$
requires $1 - \subsidy_1 \le 0 - \subsidy_i$, and hence
\[
  \subsidy_1 \;\ge\; \subsidy_i + 1 .
\]
Envy-freeness for agent $i$ with respect to $j$ requires
$1 - \subsidy_i \le -\subsidy_j$, and hence
\[
  \subsidy_i \;\ge\; \subsidy_j + 1 .
\]
Combining the two inequalities gives $\subsidy_1 \ge \subsidy_j + 2$, which is
impossible for $\subsidy \in \set{0,1}^3$. Hence no assignment of the
additional chore $c$, even after an arbitrary permutation of the previously
allocated bundles, preserves the unit-subsidy guarantee.

Notice that this is an obstruction to the incremental construction, not to the
instance itself. The complete allocation $(\set a, \set b, \set c)$ is
envy-free without subsidies: agent $1$ evaluates every singleton at cost $0$,
while agents $2$ and $3$ evaluate every singleton at cost $1$.
\end{example}
